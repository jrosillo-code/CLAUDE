# Plan, September 2026: AI operations for Spanish professional firms

*Executable version of the recommendation in `money-research-2026-09.md`, revised 12 September
2026 after independent review. Launch materials are in `launch-kit/`.*

## Context

The September 2026 research (`docs/money-research-2026-09.md`) and an independent
ChatGPT review converged on the same answer: a services-to-product company selling
workflow automation to Spanish firms that manage other businesses' paperwork, with
accounting firms (Verifactu deadline, January and July 2027) as the beachhead. One new fact
changes the opening move: the founder's father owns an insurance brokerage, Rosillo
Hermanos. That gives a design partner, real workflows and data, a first case study, and a
warm path into the broker associations. Insurance has the buyer; accounting has the clock.
The plan runs both for four weeks with the same interview guide and lets buyer behavior
pick the lead.

Constraints: solo, full-time, 3 to 9 months of runway, directs Claude Code, Spanish and
English. Objective by day 90: one workflow that three independent firms have paid to
solve, roughly €15k to €30k cumulative, one repeatable implementation, one channel.

## Positioning

One company, one buyer type: "Operaciones con IA para corredurías y asesorías." Not an
invoicing app, not a chatbot. The product is the chain receive, extract, validate, compare,
flag missing, create task, update the management system, draft the reply, human approves.
Every build ships with the compliance kit: AI disclosure (AI Act Article 50), a human
review step, an activity log, output traceability, a data policy.

Three offers, one page in Spanish and English:
- Audit, €1.5k to €3k, credited toward implementation. Map the firm, list 5 to 10
  workflows, size the hours, prototype the best one.
- Sprint, two weeks, €6k to €12k. One workflow in production with tests, docs, training.
- Managed AI operations, €2k to €6k per month. Run and extend what was built.

## Phase 0, week 1: set up to sell

- Convert Waypoint into the portfolio piece: open-source, case study built around the
  tested RLS and migration chain and the never-fabricates Claude layer, three-minute demo,
  seeded deploy stays live. No further feature work.
- Publish the one-page offer site. Join the Claude Partner Network; book the Claude
  Certified Architect Foundations exam.
- Sign a written pilot with Rosillo Hermanos: six weeks, one workflow, named contact,
  agreed access to the management system and documents, a real if nominal price. A favor
  proves nothing; a scoped pilot does.
- Build the interview guide once (the ChatGPT question set, ending with "if I took your 50
  messiest clients and made them compliant without your staff chasing, what is that worth")
  and use it unchanged on both tracks.

## Phase 1, weeks 1 to 4: two discovery tracks

Track A, insurance (design partner in hand):
- Week 1 inside the brokerage. Shadow staff, inventory every recurring task, count hours
  per week, note which system each touches. Candidates: renewal chasing, claims document
  collection, quote comparison across insurers, commission reconciliation against insurer
  statements, receipt collection, WhatsApp and email intake.
- Pick the one workflow that is most repeated, most manual, and writes into the management
  system. Weeks 2 to 4: build it as the pilot with the compliance kit.
- In parallel, ten conversations with unrelated corredurías via the father's network and
  the broker associations. Goal: one paid audit or sprint outside the family by week 4.

Track B, accounting (deadline in hand):
- Fifteen gestoría and asesoría conversations, mixed sizes, from warm network and
  professional bodies. Demo on sample invoices: intake, extraction, validation, flag what
  would fail Verifactu. Goal: two paid audits or one sprint by week 4.
- Never build an invoicing engine; integrate a certified one. Sell readiness and
  migration, which pay whether or not the deadline moves again.

Cash floor, both tracks: pitch Claude Code enablement workshops to Spanish software houses
with unused seats, €2.5k to €5k each, capped at one day a week. Record them; the recordings
seed the audience.

## Week 4 decision gate

Score each track on paid engagements outside the family, urgency in the buyers' own words,
stated willingness to pay, and ease of access.
- Track A has a working pilot and at least one unrelated correduría paying: lead with
  insurance, keep accounting as second product for the 2027 wave.
- Track B has two or more paying and Track A does not: lead with accounting.
- Both qualify: lead with insurance (buyer access compounds faster), schedule accounting
  for Q4 2026.
- Neither: run the services stack, re-run the gate at week 8.

## Phase 2, weeks 5 to 12: second and third customer, same workflow

- Sell the exact workflow from the lead track to two unrelated firms as sprints. Keep 80
  percent identical, make the 20 percent configurable. Do not accept a different workflow
  from customer three to win the deal.
- Pricing hypotheses to test, never per seat: setup €5k to €15k, then per managed
  client-company per month or per processed document, retainer above €250 per month, annual
  billing by default.
- Start the dataset from day one: document types, extraction errors, corrections,
  exceptions, turnaround times. This is the asset.
- Day 90 checkpoint: three paying firms, one workflow, one channel, one repeatable
  implementation. If met, productize. If not, the services stack is the business for now.

## Phase 3, months 4 to 9: productize

- Turn the repeated implementation into per-client software. Hire a Spanish-speaking
  implementer at nearshore rates when retainers exceed your hours.
- Insurance channel: broker associations, networks, and the management-system vendors'
  partner programs. Accounting channel: the Verifactu wave in H2 2026 and H1 2027.
- Month 6 review: decide whether the small-company tier is funded by retainers or whether
  services remain the business.

## Rules

- Sell scope, never hours. Fifty percent up front.
- No build without a buyer. The second identical build becomes the product.
- The family pilot is a workflow discovery, not market evidence.
- No Waypoint feature work unless a client pays for it.
- No plugins, skills, MCP servers or developer tools as products. Read the Claude Code
  changelog monthly for what is about to go native.
- Re-verify every number from the research before quoting it to a client.

## Kill and pivot criteria

- Week 4: neither track produced a paid engagement outside the family. Stay on services,
  re-run the gate at week 8, and start P2 discovery (WhatsApp booking plus compliant
  invoicing for small tourism operators) as the third track.
- Week 12: fewer than three independent payers for one workflow. Do not productize; keep
  selling sprints until the count is three.

## The software

The company's app lives in `ops/` (Next.js, Supabase, Anthropic SDK). As of 13
September 2026 it has: the public site on its own design system (`ops/DESIGN.md`) with
the compliance kit as a page; the document chain with the review queue; the settlement
reconciliation with a claim to the insurer behind an approval; per-firm settings
(retention, budget alert, baseline hours, insurer mailboxes); the pilot report at
`/app/{firm}/informe` that produces the two pages the agreement promises from the
activity log; CSV exports of the log, corrections and extractions; an origin check on
member sessions; 58 unit tests and 32 row-level-security assertions. `ops/README.md`
explains how to run it keyless and how to go live. What it does not have yet: a real
management-system adapter (the CSV export adapter stands in until the pilot names the
system), a second workflow, and a customer. The evidence behind Track A is in
`insurance-brokerage-research-2026-09.md`; the design brief behind the site is in
`website-design-research-2026-09.md`.
